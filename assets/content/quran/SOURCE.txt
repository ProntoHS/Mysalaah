difference matters, so they are set out separately. Each text also carries its own SOURCE.json,
which is what salaah/sources.py reads -- the screen cannot drift from the files because it is a
reading of the files.

WHY THE BENGALI HAS NO VERSION, AND WHY IT CAME ANYWAY

Every other Encyclopedia text here came from their official export, which states its revision
and publishes an address that answers whether that revision is still current. Bengali has no
export. Not XML, not CSV, not Excel, not SQLite, not ePub, not PDF: every one of
quranenc.com/en/home/d/<format>/bengali_zakaria answers with an empty page, while the same
addresses return real files for english_saheeh and portuguese_nasr -- checked against those as
a control rather than assumed. 75 of their 163 translations have an export and all three of
their Bengali editions are in the 88 that do not.

Their API does serve it, so that is where this came from, and it is their own published API
rather than a scrape of pages they chose not to publish as data. What it costs is the thing the
section below is about: the API states no revision at all. There is no version in bn's
SOURCE.json and no check_updates address, so this is the one text on the mat that cannot be
asked whether it has been corrected. That is a real gap and the note in its SOURCE.json says
so. Asking the Encyclopedia to publish a Bengali export is the fix, and it belongs in the same
letter as the other questions for them.

Until then the alternative was worse. Before this, a child praying in Bengali was shown the
Arabic with no meaning under it at all, and the Qur'an screen offered Bengali nothing.

WHY FIVE OF THEM MOVED, IN OCTOBER 2026

Until then all five translations came through quran-json, which redistributes from Tanzil and
from the Noble Qur'an Encyclopedia. Two things were wrong with that, neither of them about the
words.

Permission. The Noble Qur'an Encyclopedia gave permission for their content to be used in this
device, including a device that is sold, provided the content is not sold separately. Tanzil
gives permission for non-commercial use only and says plainly that anything else has to be
asked of the translator or the publisher. So the texts that are theirs -- the French, the Urdu
and the transliteration -- are used here on that basis and no other, and the mat is not for
sale.

Versions. Tanzil does not version its editions. The Encyclopedia versions every one of theirs
and publishes an address that answers whether a version is still current; both are recorded in
each SOURCE.json as check_updates. A text that cannot be asked whether it has been corrected
cannot be kept correct, and this mat is meant to sit in a child's room for years.

WHAT CHANGED IN THE WORDS

Measured verse by verse against what was there before, after taking off the footnote marks and
the verse numbers described below:

    en    5069 of 6236 verses read exactly the same; 1167 changed
    es    5531 of 6236 the same;  705 changed
    zh    6234 of 6236 the same;    2 changed

Every one is the same translation by the same translator, at the publisher's newer revision.
The English changes are mostly added glosses -- "His verses [i.e., laws]" where it used to read
"His verses" -- and a macron on Allah. Al-Fatiha 7 now reads "those who have earned [Your]
anger" where it read "evoked". The Spanish differences are refinements of the same kind. The
two Chinese verses are 78:24 and 81:1.

Hindi and Portuguese are new. There was no Hindi or Portuguese Qur'an on the mat before, which
is why quran.py offered Hindi from 1.84 with nothing behind it.

TWO THINGS STRIPPED AT THE POINT OF DISPLAY, NOT IN THE FILES

The files here are byte-for-byte the publisher's export, because their terms ask that the text
not be modified. What the screen shows is narrower, and salaah/quran.py:as_shown does it:

    [12]   a footnote reference. The footnotes themselves are NOT on the mat. There are 6,632
           of them across the five editions -- 1613 in English, 1011 in Spanish, 2036 in Hindi,
           1972 in Portuguese, none in Chinese -- and 1.7 MB of text that nothing on this mat
           can display is 1.7 MB of every SD card spent on nothing. The marks are left in the
           text because the text is the publisher's, and stripping them at the point of display
           keeps the files theirs. Nothing is lost: tools/import_quranenc.py pulls the
           footnotes out of the same export, and each SOURCE.json carries the address the
           export came from. The day there is a screen that can show one, they take an hour to
           put back.
    "7. "  a verse number at the head of the verse. The Spanish export does this and the others
           do not, and the reader already prints the number in the margin. It is taken off only
           when the number IS that verse's number, which is what stops it eating a verse that
           opens with a numeral. 5:88 in the Spanish is written "88," with a comma rather than
           a full stop, so both are allowed for.

A NUMBER in brackets goes. The translators' own brackets stay: "[All] praise is [due] to Allah"
is Saheeh International writing, not an annotation, and taking those out would take words out of
scripture.

tests/test_core.py writes that rule out a second time rather than importing it, for the reason
given in DuaContentTest -- two implementations agree only when both are right.

WHAT IS CHECKED, AND WHAT IS NOT

tools/check_quran.py proves the shape: 114 surahs, every one the length it has been for
centuries, 49,888 verses across the Arabic and the seven translations, every verse present and
numbered, none blank. It finds the translations by looking in this folder rather than from a
list, because the list it used to carry said five and went on saying five after Hindi and
Portuguese arrived.

It does not prove the words, and nothing here can. Every SOURCE.json says reviewed: false and
will go on saying so until somebody who reads Arabic has been over it.

What those files now also say is publisher_reviewed, and the distinction is deliberate. A text
issued by a house with a review process is not the same thing as a text nobody has looked at,
and neither is the same as a text this project's own reader has approved -- which, for every
word of scripture on this mat, is still none of them. The Sources page says which of the three
each text is, in those words.
